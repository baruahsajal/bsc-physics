1. Discovery of the Cell

Cells were discovered by Robert Hooke in 1665 using a primitive microscope. He observed cork slices and coined the term "cell" (meaning little rooms). Leeuwenhoek later discovered free-living living cells in pond water.

1.1 Cell Theory

Cell Theory: Proposed by Schleiden and Schwann (and later expanded by Virchow), stating that all plants and animals are composed of cells and that all cells arise from pre-existing cells.

2. Cellular Structure & Organelles

A eukaryotic cell consists of three major components: the plasma membrane, the nucleus, and the cytoplasm containing specialized membrane-bound organelles.

  • Mitochondria: Known as the powerhouses of the cell; generate chemical energy stored as ATP.
  • Chloroplasts: Found exclusively in plant cells; sites of photosynthesis containing chlorophyll.
  • Ribosomes: Sites of protein synthesis, suspended in cytoplasm or attached to the endoplasmic reticulum.
