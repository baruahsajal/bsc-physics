Motion

Course Code: SCI-PHY-01 | Class 9 Science Academy

1. Describing Motion

An object is considered to be in motion if its position changes with time relative to a reference point (observer or origin). Motion can be along a straight line (rectilinear), circular, or oscillatory.

1.1 Scalar and Vector Quantities

Distance vs. Displacement: Distance is the total path length covered by an object (scalar quantity), whereas displacement is the shortest path between the initial and final positions (vector quantity).

2. Speed and Velocity

Speed is the rate of motion (distance traveled per unit time), while velocity is speed in a specified direction.

$$ \text{Average Velocity} = \frac{u + v}{2} $$ $$ \text{Acceleration } (a) = \frac{v - u}{t} $$

Where $u$ is the initial velocity, $v$ is the final velocity, and $t$ is time taken.

3. Equations of Motion

For an object moving with uniform acceleration along a straight line, the kinematic equations relating velocity, time, acceleration, and displacement are:

$$ v = u + at $$ $$ s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 $$ $$ v^2 = u^2 + 2as $$
