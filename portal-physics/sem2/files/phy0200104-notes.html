1. Differential Equations in Physics

Many physical systems are governed by differential equations. In electricity and magnetism, potentials and fields satisfy ordinary and partial differential equations such as Laplace's and Poisson's equations.

1.1 Linear Differential Equations with Constant Coefficients

A second-order homogeneous linear differential equation has the general form:

$$ \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + a \frac{dy}{dx} + b y = 0 $$

The auxiliary equation is given by $m^2 + am + b = 0$, determining the nature of oscillations in LRC circuits.

2. Electrostatics & Gauss's Law

2.1 Electric Field and Potential

Electrostatic Potential ($V$): The work done per unit positive charge in bringing it from infinity to a point in an electric field.

The relation between electric field $\vec{E}$ and potential $V$ is:

$$ \vec{E} = -\nabla V $$

2.2 Gauss's Law in Differential Form

Gauss's Law states that the total electric flux out of a closed surface is proportional to the total charge enclosed within the surface:

$$ \nabla \cdot \vec{E} = \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0} $$
